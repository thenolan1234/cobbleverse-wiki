Regions & World

World generation, structures and add-on packs

COBBLEVERSE plays out across a reshaped Minecraft overworld — and, for the bold, the Nether and the End. The base install generates vanilla biomes seeded with Kanto gyms, legendary monuments, Poké Center villages and raid dens, all injected by datapacks that the Global Packs mod loads into every world automatically. Three more regions (Johto, Hoenn, Sinnoh) and the Terralith terrain overhaul sit in an extra folder, one file-move away from being part of your adventure. This page explains what generates where, what each optional pack really contains, and how to switch things on and off.

How the world generates

Out of the box the overworld uses the vanilla biome layout — Terralith is not active by default. It ships only as an optional datapack (datapacks/extra/Terralith-DP.zip); there is no Terralith mod jar, and the pre-installed Biome Replacer config only fires on terralith: biomes, so it does nothing until you enable that pack.

Vanilla doesn't mean untouched, though. The main datapack (COBBLEVERSE-DP-v31.zip) ships modified copies of 35 vanilla biome definitions plus custom overworld noise settings and density functions, so terrain shape and biome decoration are quietly retuned even before any add-on. The same pack carries the bulk of the Pokémon world data: 1,024 wild spawn files and 376 species additions layered on top of Cobblemon 1.7.3 — which is why Pokémon from later generations already roam the wild without any region pack enabled — along with all the Kanto structures below, 266 raid den encounter definitions and the fossil set.

Everything in the datapacks/ folder is force-loaded into every world (singleplayer or new saves alike) by the Global Packs mod — see the enabling section for the exact config.

Villages, Poké Centers and raid dens

Villages are overhauled. The pack rewrites vanilla village placement (spacing 50 / separation 25 chunks, versus vanilla's 34 / 8 — so villages are more spread out) and stacks the village pool in favour of custom villages from the Cobblemon Additions mod: its small/mid/large villages roll at weights 6/4/2 while each vanilla village type keeps weight 1. The custom villages draw from a special one-off building pool that includes a full Poké Center, a Poké Mart, a battle pad and a lodge, so it's worth walking every street. Swamp huts are similarly mostly replaced by a custom witch hut variant.

Every vanilla-style village gets a Poké Center too. Cobblemon itself generates a Poké Center building with a healing machine in villages; the PokeCenterPCs-DP.zip datapack swaps in 19 rebuilt versions whose stated purpose is to put a PC block in every village Poké Center. The Pokeblocks mod supplies additional decorative blocks used around the pack's builds.

Raid dens come from the Cobblemon Raid Dens mod, configured for natural spawning at a 1-in-480 chance roll. Dens span tiers 1–7 with the odds weighted toward tiers 3–5 (tier 7 is a 2% roll), and each den resets every 2 hours of game time, rerolling both boss and tier. Rewards are distributed by damage dealt. The main datapack registers 266 raid encounters and boss pools covering the pack's dex plus G-Max, Mega, legendary, mythical, paradox and Ultra Beast categories, with two den structure variants (basic and Dynamax).

Kanto landmarks in every world

The main datapack adds 19 jigsaw structures to worldgen. Each gym generates in one signature biome — and the matching gym leader spawns using a biome tag that mirrors it, so finding the biome finds both:

  • Brock — plains (leader)
  • Misty — lukewarm ocean (leader; a shiny variant of the build exists)
  • Lt. Surge — savanna plateau (leader)
  • Erika — flower forest (leader)
  • Koga — swamp (leader)
  • Sabrina — dark forest (leader)
  • Blaine — the Nether's crimson forest (leader)
  • Giovanni — generates in the same biomes as ancient cities, deep underground in the deep dark (leader)

Overworld gyms use spacing 40 chunks, so one is never far. Beyond the gyms:

  • Kanto League tower — End highlands: the Pokémon League awaits in the End (spacing 120).
  • Ash's structure — plains, at triple gym rarity (spacing 120).
  • Team Rocket Tower — badlands (spacing 60; shiny variant exists).
  • Legendary monuments (via the bundled LegendaryMonuments mod): Articuno in snowy plains, Zapdos on stony shores, Moltres in the Nether wastes (all spacing 151), and Mew deep in jungles — at spacing 251, the rarest structure in the base pack.
  • Crown Spire (snowy plains) and Crown Cemetery (old-growth pine taiga) at spacing 180, plus Dawn Tower and Dusk Tower in the End highlands at spacing 200.

Biomes and rarity above are read straight from the pack's worldgen files. What each building holds is only hinted at by its file name — expect the trainer or legendary the name promises, but treat interiors as yours to discover.

Optional add-ons: Johto, Hoenn, Sinnoh, Terralith

Four zips wait in datapacks/extra/. Note what they don't contain: no wild spawn or trainer-team files — those already ship in the base pack (the Trainers datapack defines every Johto, Hoenn and Sinnoh leader, Elite Four and champion, and the main datapack already spawns their Pokémon). What the region packs add are the structures, region advancements, recipes/trades, and the biome tags that let those leaders actually appear in the world.

  • COBBLEVERSE-Johto-DP.zip — 9 gym/league structures (the Johto League tower generates in the End highlands, like Kanto's) plus landmark structures: Bell Tower (forest), Burned Tower (birch forest), Celebi Shrine (taiga), Whirl Island (deep cold ocean) and the Rocket Radio Tower (savanna). 21 advancements including catching Ho-Oh, Lugia and Celebi. Every placement targets a vanilla biome, so Johto works with or without Terralith.
  • COBBLEVERSE-Hoenn-DP.zip — 9 gyms plus a league complex, and a dense legendary map: Groudon (warm ocean), Kyogre (deep cold ocean), Regirock/Regice/Registeel, Jirachi, Deoxys (small End islands), the Sky Pillar (deep ocean), a Secret Garden and a Dyna Tree. 33 advancements. Most placements target terralith: biomes, so Hoenn is built to run alongside the Terralith pack — it even ships adjusted Terralith loot tables.
  • COBBLEVERSE-Sinnoh-DP.zip — 9 gyms plus a league castle, Spear Pillar, Snowpoint Temple, Fullmoon Island, Crescent Isle, Flower Paradise, Split Decision Temple, Team Galactic HQ, the Eterna building and a wind plant. 50 advancements covering Dialga, Palkia, Giratina, Arceus, Darkrai, Manaphy and more. Like Hoenn, most placements target terralith: biomes — pair it with Terralith.
  • Terralith-DP.zip — Stardust Labs' "Terralith – Overworld Evolved": 95 custom biomes and 28 structures of its own (mage towers, fortified villages, glacial huts and more). The pack pre-configures the Biome Replacer mod (config/biome_replacer.properties) to swap 32 of Terralith's more exotic biomes — skylands, alpha islands, yellowstone and others — back to vanilla equivalents; those rules only apply while Terralith is active, and none of them touch the biomes the Hoenn or Sinnoh structures need.

How to enable the add-ons

This pack loads datapacks globally through the Global Packs mod. Its config (config/global_packs.toml) declares two folders: everything directly in datapacks/ is required — force-loaded into every world — while zips in datapacks/extra/ are registered as optional packs, offered in the world's data pack selection rather than auto-enabled.

The simplest, always-works method:

  • Close the game.
  • Move the zip (e.g. COBBLEVERSE-Johto-DP.zip) from datapacks/extra/ into datapacks/.
  • Relaunch — the pack now loads automatically in every world, new or existing.

Two things to know when adding packs to an existing world: new structures and biomes only appear in chunks generated after the pack is active — terrain you've already explored keeps its old generation — and enabling Terralith mid-save will produce visible seams where old chunks meet new. Sensible combinations, based on where each pack's structures generate: Johto any time after Kanto; enable Terralith together with (or before) Hoenn and Sinnoh, since their gyms and monuments target Terralith biomes that otherwise never exist.

Variant packs: No Ender Dragon, No Hunger

Two small toggle packs sit directly in datapacks/ — which, under the Global Packs config above, means both are active in every world by default. To turn one off, move its zip out of the datapacks/ folder while the game is closed.

  • COBBLEVERSE - No Ender Dragon.zip — runs a function every tick that sets any newly appeared Ender Dragon's max health and health to 0. The dragon dies the instant it exists; the End itself is untouched. Since the Kanto and Johto Leagues and the Dawn/Dusk towers all generate in the End, this lets you head straight to the League without a dragon fight.
  • COBBLEVERSE - No Hunger.zip — applies a hidden Saturation effect to every player every tick, so the hunger bar never drains. Food still works; eating just becomes optional.

Both packs are single-function tick loops with no other content, so removing them has no side effects beyond restoring the vanilla mechanic.

Unlocking Regions: Kanto → Johto → Hoenn → Sinnoh

Every region already ships inside the modpack — nothing extra to download or install. Only Kanto is active in a fresh world; Johto, Hoenn and Sinnoh sit in the pack's datapacks/extra/ folder as disabled optional datapacks (COBBLEVERSE-Johto-DP, COBBLEVERSE-Hoenn-DP, COBBLEVERSE-Sinnoh-DP, plus a Terralith-DP terrain pack that comes online with Hoenn). The Global Packs mod attaches these to every world automatically, new or existing, so never copy datapack files into a world's own folder by hand — progression flips them on for you.

The normal unlock loop, one region at a time:

  • Beat the region in order. Defeat all eight Gym Leaders, the Elite Four, then the Champion: Blue (Kanto), Lance (Johto), Rocco (Hoenn), Camilla (Sinnoh). The Trainer Association series data hard-requires the previous region, so you cannot start Johto's ladder before finishing Kanto's.
  • The Champion win does the unlocking. Beating the Champion grants an advancement whose reward function enables the next region's datapack and hands the winner a champion reward plus an in-game guide book explaining the next steps.
  • Restart once. The newly enabled datapack only loads when the world is closed and reopened (on a server, the host reboots it). After the restart the next region's structures, crafting recipes and legendary content start generating — the pack's own book confirms no Pokémon, items or world data are lost. Unlocks are per-world: only the first player to beat the Champion triggers it, once.
  • Switch your own series at the Trainer Association. Region unlock is world-wide, but your personal progression is not: trade your Trainer Card at the Trainer Association for the next region's card. This resets your level cap (to 25 entering Johto or Hoenn, 35 entering Sinnoh) without affecting other players — see Mechanics for how level caps work.

Skipping ahead with commands. Per FryBry's unlock tutorial (commands verified against the pack's function files): with cheats enabled or server op, run /function cobbleverse:blue_champion_defeated to instantly unlock Johto — it grants the Kanto champion reward and enables the Johto datapack without marking Kanto's trainers as beaten; restart the world after. The chain cannot be jumped, because each skip command lives inside the datapack the previous one enables: after the restart run /function cobbleverse:lance_champion_defeated for Hoenn, restart again, then /function cobbleverse:rocco_champion_defeated for Sinnoh. The skip only unlocks the world — to update your own card and level cap afterwards, visit the Trainer Association or run /function help:johto_series (likewise hoenn_series / sinnoh_series).

Version notes for older packs. Per Ploop Craft's 1.7.40–1.7.42 rundown, patches land quickly and fix progression-adjacent bugs, so update before troubleshooting a stuck unlock — changelogs are one click away via the version number on the title screen. Notably, before 1.7.41 the shiny Rayquaza that replaces the Ender Dragon could despawn before you arrived, leaving the End empty; it now has despawn protection, which matters because Kanto's endgame — including the Champion Blue fight — takes place in the End. Sinnoh is currently the last link in the chain (its champion function unlocks no further region yet), with the Unova update in development.
